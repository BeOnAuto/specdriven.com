# First Principles

![First Principles](/images/pages/first-principles/index.png)

Everything else on this site builds on a small set of foundational ideas. This section lays them out, in order, with no jargon and no hurry.

If you've read the [Manifesto](/manifesto), the [Why](/why), and [Common Objections](/objections), this is where the underlying thinking lives. The rest of the site (the [Guides](/guides/), the [Landscape](/landscape/), the [Perspectives](/perspectives/the-spec-driven-shift/)) all draw on these five ideas.

Read in order, or jump to the one you need.

## The Five

[**The Nature of Information Systems**](/first-principles/information-systems). Why information has always been the substrate of every system humans have built, and why specifications exist because designed mechanisms around information need their designs communicated.

[**Value, Quality, and Throughput**](/first-principles/value-quality-throughput). The triangle every team is balancing whether they name it or not. Why quality wraps around value, why throughput is value delivered (not stuff produced), and why specs sit at the centre.

[**The Feedback Principle**](/first-principles/feedback-principle). The whole theory of quality in software in one sentence: the sooner you detect feedback, the cheaper it is to deal with. Quality functions, the cost curve, shifting left, and the feedback opportunity pattern that recurs across the Practice section.

[**The Lifecycle of a Specification**](/first-principles/spec-lifecycle). Specs are born, refined, changed, split, and die. The lifecycle decides what makes a healthy spec inventory and what creates spec debt.

[**The Fractal Nature of Design**](/first-principles/fractal-design). Requirements, solutions and specifications are not phases. They're the same activity recurring at every zoom level. The fractal lens resolves several common arguments about TDD, BDD, waterfall, and where AI fits.
